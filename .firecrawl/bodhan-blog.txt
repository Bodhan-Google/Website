# 1\. Why Another ASR Model?

Talk about the challenges.

Possible points:

- India has many languages.
- Existing ASR systems perform well only on high-resource languages.
- Dialects remain underserved.
- Code-mixed speech is common.
- Production systems need low latency.

End with

Bodhan ASR was built to address these challenges in a single multilingual model.

# Additional "mini-blogs" or callout sections (recommended)

These can be embedded throughout the main post to make it more engaging:

1. Why Code-Mixed Transcription Matters: Explain why Indian conversations naturally mix English with regional languages and show before/after examples.
2. Supporting Low-Resource Languages: Highlight Bhili (and potentially Bhojpuri) with a short discussion of why these languages are often underrepresented and how Bodhan Scribe addresses that gap.
3. Scaling with Synthetic Data: Describe how the 40K hours of zero-shot TTS data improved vocabulary diversity and robustness, ideally with examples of rare names or domain-specific terms.
4. Engineering for Production: Showcase offline inference, streaming, TensorRT acceleration, batching, and latency/throughput trade-offs for deployment scenarios.

* * *

## Overall Flow

The complete narrative should feel like this:

The Challenge → Introducing Bodhan Scribe → What Makes It Different → How It Works → How It Was Trained → Benchmark Results → Real Transcription Examples → Production Performance → Model Card & Limitations → Getting Started

This structure balances storytelling with technical depth, keeps readers engaged, and naturally surfaces your strongest differentiators—broad multilingual coverage, multiple transcription formats, low-resource language support, extensive benchmarking, and production-ready deployment.
